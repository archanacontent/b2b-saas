Competitive Intelligence

Sales teams often hear about a rival's new price or feature for the first time from a buyer on a call. Without a prepared answer, the rep hesitates, and the buyer reads that hesitation as weakness. Deals rarely turn on one moment, but a pattern of them shows up in the win rate within a quarter.

Competitive intelligence closes the gap. You track what rivals sell and what they charge, then turn the findings into tools a rep can use in the middle of a conversation. The same work shows marketing where your positioning has room to stand apart.

You'll leave with a competitor tracker, a battlecard for your most frequent rival and a comparison sheet you can share with buyers.

Competitor Tracker

Pricing pages change quietly. In September 2026, Pipedrive's own page listed its Premium plan at $59 a seat while six third-party guides still showed $49. Record every figure with the date you checked it and a link to the source, then recheck prices monthly and the full tracker each quarter.

Battlecard

A battlecard has one job: to give a rep a confident answer in under a minute. Keep it to a single page, and write it from what reps hear on calls, so it covers the situations they actually meet.

Their pitch

The rival's promise in one sentence, as a buyer would repeat it.

When we win

The buyer situations where your product is the better fit.

When we lose

The situations where the rival is stronger, stated honestly.

Landmine questions

Questions a rep can ask early that expose the rival's weak spots.

Objections

The common pushbacks, each with a short, factual response.

Proof

Customer stories, reviews or data that back your claims.

Comparison Sheet

A comparison sheet goes to the buyer, so every line in it has to survive a check. Include at least one row where the rival comes out ahead. Buyers trust a sheet that admits a weakness far more than one that wins every line.

Battlecard: Freemium Marketing-Led CRM

Their pitch

"Start free and grow on one platform."

When we win

The team has outgrown the free tier and needs invoicing and support in the same system as sales.

When we lose

Marketing already runs on their platform, or the buyer wants the largest library of integrations.

Landmine questions

"What will you pay once you add five more sales seats and forecasting?" and "How many tools does a deal pass through before the invoice goes out?"

Objections

"The free plan covers us." It does for now, but check the price at the next plan up before the team depends on it.

Proof

Customer stories from the first users in the new market, to be added after launch.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Feature-by-feature tables - Long checklists bury the two or three differences a buyer cares about.
  • Running down rivals - Buyers distrust a vendor that attacks competitors, so stick to facts they can check.
  • Stale battlecards - A card that quotes last year's prices costs the rep credibility the first time a buyer corrects it.
  • Forgetting "do nothing" - Spreadsheets and the status quo win more deals than any named rival, and they need their own answers.
