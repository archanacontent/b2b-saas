# Competitive Intelligence

Sales teams often hear about a rival's new price or feature for the first time from a buyer on a call. Without a prepared answer, the rep hesitates, and the buyer reads that hesitation as weakness. Deals rarely turn on one moment, but a pattern of them shows up in the win rate within a quarter.

Competitive intelligence closes the gap. You track what rivals sell and what they charge, then turn the findings into tools a rep can use in the middle of a conversation. The same work shows marketing where your positioning has room to stand apart.

You'll leave with a competitor tracker, a battlecard for your most frequent rival and a comparison sheet you can share with buyers.

## Competitor Tracker

Pricing pages change quietly. In September 2026, Pipedrive's own page listed its Premium plan at $59 a seat while [six third-party guides still showed $49](https://www.saasswitcher.com/blog/pipedrive-pricing). Record every figure with the date you checked it and a link to the source, then recheck prices monthly and the full tracker each quarter.

| Field | What to capture |
| :--- | :--- |
| Competitor | Vendor name and the product you meet in deals. |
| Target buyer | The company size and role their marketing speaks to. |
| Core message | Their main promise, in their own words from the homepage. |
| Pricing | Plan names and prices, with the date checked. |
| Strengths | What buyers praise in reviews and sales calls. |
| Weaknesses | What buyers complain about, quoted where possible. |
| Recent moves | Launches, price changes, acquisitions and new partners. |
| Source and date | A link for every claim and the date you confirmed it. |

## Battlecard

A battlecard has one job: to give a rep a confident answer in under a minute. Keep it to a single page, and write it from what reps hear on calls, so it covers the situations they actually meet.

| Section | What to include |
| :--- | :--- |
| Their pitch | The rival's promise in one sentence, as a buyer would repeat it. |
| When we win | The buyer situations where your product is the better fit. |
| When we lose | The situations where the rival is stronger, stated honestly. |
| Landmine questions | Questions a rep can ask early that expose the rival's weak spots. |
| Objections | The common pushbacks, each with a short, factual response. |
| Proof | Customer stories, reviews or data that back your claims. |

## Comparison Sheet

A comparison sheet goes to the buyer, so every line in it has to survive a check. Include at least one row where the rival comes out ahead. Buyers trust a sheet that admits a weakness far more than one that wins every line.

## Worked Example

### Competitor Tracker

| Field | Enterprise CRM | Freemium marketing-led CRM | Pipeline-first sales CRM |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Target buyer | Large sales teams with dedicated admins | Small teams that start with marketing tools | Small sales teams that want a simple pipeline |
| Core message | One platform for every customer team | Start free, grow on one platform | See your deals and close them faster |
| Pricing | Per-seat tiers, with heavy add-on costs | A free tier, with costs that rise steeply at higher plans | Low entry price per seat |
| Strengths | Deep customisation and integrations | Easy start and strong marketing tools | Fast setup and a clean interface |
| Weaknesses | Needs an admin and a long setup | Costs jump once the team needs reporting or more seats | Limited outside sales, with no invoicing or help desk |

## Mistakes To Avoid

- **Feature-by-feature tables** - Long checklists bury the two or three differences a buyer cares about.
- **Running down rivals** - Buyers distrust a vendor that attacks competitors, so stick to facts they can check.
- **Stale battlecards** - A card that quotes last year's prices costs the rep credibility the first time a buyer corrects it.
- **Forgetting "do nothing"** - Spreadsheets and the status quo win more deals than any named rival, and they need their own answers.
