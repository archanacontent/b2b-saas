# Launch Content Kit

Launch content usually gets written last and in a hurry. Each writer works from a different brief, so the product page promises one outcome, the solution brief another, and the announcement leads with a feature nobody asked about. Sales then builds its own one-pager because nothing official arrived in time.

A content kit fixes the order. Every asset is built from the message hierarchy, drafted in the pre-launch window and approved together, so a buyer meets the same promise on the website, in the inbox and on a sales call.

You'll leave with templates for a product page, a solution brief, a brochure, a case study, a launch announcement and industry-specific collateral.

## Product Page

The product page is where most buyers form their first view, often before anyone from sales has spoken to them. Lead with the outcome from your headline promise, and give each value pillar its own section with proof directly beneath it.

| Section | What it holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| Hero | Headline promise, one supporting line and the primary call to action. |
| Problem | The buyer's pain in two or three sentences, in their language. |
| Value pillars | One section per pillar, each with a product visual and a proof point. |
| How it works | The first three steps a new customer takes. |
| Proof | Customer logos, a short quote or a review rating. |
| Pricing cue | A starting price or a link to the pricing page. |
| Closing call to action | The same action as the hero, repeated once. |

## Solution Brief And One-Pager

A solution brief is a two-page document a champion can forward to the decision-maker. The one-pager is its shorter sibling, built for a rep to leave behind after a call. Both should answer the questions the founder or the finance lead will ask without the champion in the room.

| Section | What it holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| The problem | The cost of the current situation, ideally as a number. |
| The solution | What the product does, tied to that cost. |
| Outcomes | Two or three results, each with proof. |
| How it fits | Setup time, integrations and what changes for the team. |
| Next step | The trial, demo or contact to take things forward. |

## Brochure

The brochure covers the whole suite rather than a single product, for events, partners and buyers comparing vendors. Keep the structure of the solution brief, and add a section showing how the products connect.

## Case Study

A case study works when it reads as the customer's story rather than the vendor's. Get permission early, interview the customer and the user who works in the product daily, and lead with the result.

| Section | What it holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| Result | The headline outcome, with a number. |
| Customer | Company, size, industry and the team involved. |
| Challenge | What wasn't working, in the customer's words. |
| Why they chose you | The alternatives they compared and what decided it. |
| Implementation | How long setup took and who was involved. |
| Results | Measured outcomes with timeframes. |
| Quote | One line from the decision-maker, approved in writing. |

## Launch Announcement

The announcement goes out on launch day as a blog post, an email and LinkedIn posts. Write the blog post first, then cut the email and posts from it, so all three carry the same promise.

| Part | What it holds |
| :--- | :--- |
| Opening | The buyer problem the launch solves. |
| What's new | The product and its headline promise. |
| Why it matters | Two or three outcomes, with proof. |
| Availability | Who can get it, when, and at what price. |
| Next step | One clear call to action. |

## Industry Collateral

An industry version keeps the core positioning and swaps in the pains, workflows and proof that belong to one sector. A professional services firm and a distributor both lose deals to missed follow-ups, but they describe the problem in different words.

### Product Page

| Hero | "Every deal, from first call to paid invoice, in one CRM your team will use." Call to action: Start a 14-day trial. |

## Mistakes To Avoid

- **Content written from the feature list** - Copy that starts with features makes the buyer work out the benefit for themselves.
- **Assets drafted in isolation** - Without the message hierarchy as a shared brief, every writer invents a new promise.
- **Case studies without numbers** - A story with no measured result reads as a testimonial, and buyers weigh it accordingly.
- **Sales building its own collateral** - Rep-made slides drift from the approved message, so deliver official assets before launch week.
