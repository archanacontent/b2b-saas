Sales Enablement

A rep's first week with a new product decides how they'll sell it for the next year. If the training is a single slide deck and a recorded demo, reps fall back on the product they already know, and the launch quota misses. Pipeline from the new product stays thin, and the launch gets blamed for a problem that started in enablement.

Enablement gives reps what they need to sell with confidence from day one. It turns positioning into a deck they can present, questions that qualify a buyer quickly and answers to the objections they'll hear on every call.

You'll leave with a sales deck outline, a discovery framework, an objection-handling guide and an agenda for the launch training session.

Sales Deck Outline

The deck should tell the buyer's story before it shows the product. Ten to 12 slides is enough for a first call, with demo time and appendix slides kept separate.

Slide What it covers
1 Title, with the headline promise as the subtitle.
2 The shift in the buyer's world that makes the problem urgent now.
3 The problem, in the buyer's words, with its cost.
4 What good looks like once the problem is solved.
5 How the product gets them there, at a glance.
6 to 8 One slide per value pillar, each with proof.
9 Customer proof or a short case study.
10 Pricing and how to start.
11 Next steps, with a date.

Discovery Framework

Discovery decides whether a deal is worth pursuing, and good questions do half the selling. Ask about the situation and the cost of the problem before mentioning the product.

Stage What to learn Sample question
Situation How they work today "Walk me through what happens after a first call with a new prospect."
Problem Where it breaks "When did a deal last slip because a follow-up was missed?"
Cost What it costs them "Roughly how many deals a quarter end that way?"
Decision Who decides and how "Who else will weigh in before you choose a CRM?"
Timing Why now "What happens if this is still the setup in six months?"

Enablement Session Agenda

Run the session live, two weeks before launch, and finish with each rep practising the pitch. A recording alone won't prepare anyone for a real buyer.

Time Segment
15 min Why we built it and who it's for
20 min Positioning, pillars and proof
20 min Live demo of the buyer's main workflow
15 min Competitors and the battlecard
20 min Objections, with role-play in pairs
10 min Where to find assets and who to ask

Objection-Handling Guide

Objection What's behind it Response
"We already use a free CRM." Fear of paying for something they get free today "That's how most teams start. What does your plan cost once you add forecasting and five more seats?"
"My reps won't update another tool." A past CRM rollout that failed "The assistant logs calls and drafts follow-ups for them. Would a trial with two reps show you whether they'd use it?"
"Moving our data will take months." The cost of migration "Contacts and deals import from a spreadsheet in an afternoon. Which system holds most of your history today?"
"We've never heard of you here." Risk of choosing an unknown vendor "Fair point. Would it help to speak with a customer of a similar size from our other markets?"

Mistakes To Avoid

  • A product tour as training - Reps who only see features can't connect them to a buyer's problem on a live call.
  • One session and done - Without refreshers after the first month, reps forget the positioning and revert to their own pitch.
  • Objection answers written by marketing alone - Responses that never met a real buyer sound rehearsed, so test them with reps first.
  • Decks that start with the company - Buyers don't need your founding story on slide two. Open with their problem.
