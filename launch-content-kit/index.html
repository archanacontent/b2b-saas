Launch Content Kit

Launch content usually gets written last and in a hurry. Each writer works from a different brief, so the product page promises one outcome, the solution brief another, and the announcement leads with a feature nobody asked about. Sales then builds its own one-pager because nothing official arrived in time.

A content kit fixes the order. Every asset is built from the message hierarchy, drafted in the pre-launch window and approved together, so a buyer meets the same promise on the website, in the inbox and on a sales call.

You'll leave with templates for a product page, a solution brief, a brochure, a case study, a launch announcement and industry-specific collateral.

Product Page

The product page is where most buyers form their first view, often before anyone from sales has spoken to them. Lead with the outcome from your headline promise, and give each value pillar its own section with proof directly beneath it.

Hero

Headline promise, one supporting line and the primary call to action.

Problem

The buyer's pain in two or three sentences, in their language.

Value pillars

One section per pillar, each with a product visual and a proof point.

How it works

The first three steps a new customer takes.

Proof

Customer logos, a short quote or a review rating.

Pricing cue

A starting price or a link to the pricing page.

Closing call to action

The same action as the hero, repeated once.

Solution Brief And One-Pager

A solution brief is a two-page document a champion can forward to the decision-maker. The one-pager is its shorter sibling, built for a rep to leave behind after a call. Both should answer the questions the founder or the finance lead will ask without the champion in the room.

The problem

The cost of the current situation, ideally as a number.

The solution

What the product does, tied to that cost.

Outcomes

Two or three results, each with proof.

How it fits

Setup time, integrations and what changes for the team.

Next step

The trial, demo or contact to take things forward.

Brochure

The brochure covers the whole suite rather than a single product, for events, partners and buyers comparing vendors. Keep the structure of the solution brief, and add a section showing how the products connect.

Case Study

A case study works when it reads as the customer's story rather than the vendor's. Get permission early, interview the customer and the user who works in the product daily, and lead with the result.

Result

The headline outcome, with a number.

Customer

Company, size, industry and the team involved.

Challenge

What wasn't working, in the customer's words.

Why they chose you

The alternatives they compared and what decided it.

Implementation

How long setup took and who was involved.

Results

Measured outcomes with timeframes.

Quote

One line from the decision-maker, approved in writing.

Launch Announcement

The announcement goes out on launch day as a blog post, an email and LinkedIn posts. Write the blog post first, then cut the email and posts from it, so all three carry the same promise.

Opening

The buyer problem the launch solves.

What's new

The product and its headline promise.

Why it matters

Two or three outcomes, with proof.

Availability

Who can get it, when, and at what price.

Next step

One clear call to action.

Industry Collateral

An industry version keeps the core positioning and swaps in the pains, workflows and proof that belong to one sector. A professional services firm and a distributor both lose deals to missed follow-ups, but they describe the problem in different words.

Worked Example

The company and its content are fictional and illustrative.

Product Page

Hero

"Every deal, from first call to paid invoice, in one CRM your team will use." Call to action: Start a 14-day trial.

Problem

Your pipeline lives in spreadsheets, your invoices in another tool, and follow-ups depend on someone remembering them.

Value pillars

Deal to invoice in one place. An assistant that handles the admin. A price that holds as you grow.

How it works

Import your contacts, connect your email and invite your team.

Proof

Beta customer quotes, to be added after beta.

Pricing cue

One flat price per seat on every paid plan.

Closing call to action

Start a 14-day trial with your own pipeline.

Launch Announcement Opening

A deal often slips away quietly. The follow-up never happened, because the reminder sat in a spreadsheet nobody opened that week. Today we're launching a CRM built to catch those moments, with an AI assistant that logs every call and drafts the next email for you.

Industry Collateral: Professional Services Edition

Pain in their words

Proposals go out, then nobody tracks which ones are still open.

Workflow change

Proposals, engagements and invoices linked to one client record.

Proof needed

A firm of similar size that shortened its proposal-to-signature time.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Content written from the feature list - Copy that starts with features makes the buyer work out the benefit for themselves.
  • Assets drafted in isolation - Without the message hierarchy as a shared brief, every writer invents a new promise.
  • Case studies without numbers - A story with no measured result reads as a testimonial, and buyers weigh it accordingly.
  • Sales building its own collateral - Rep-made slides drift from the approved message, so deliver official assets before launch week.
