Go-To-Market And Launch Plan

Launches slip in the handoffs. Marketing announces a date before sales has been trained, support learns about the new pricing from the first confused customer, and nobody owns the follow-up once launch week ends. A month later, the team is arguing about whether the launch worked, with no agreed measure to settle it.

A launch plan fixes ownership and timing before the work starts. It sets how big the launch should be, what each team delivers in each phase, and which channels carry the message to the buyers defined in your ICP.

You'll leave with a launch tier that sizes the effort, a phase-by-phase checklist, a RACI chart, a timeline and a channel plan.

Launch Tier

Not every release deserves the same effort, and treating a minor update like a major launch burns budget and wears out the audience. Pick the tier first, since it decides how much of the rest of the plan you need.

Tier When to use it What it typically includes
Tier 1 A new product, a new market or a major pricing change Full campaign, press, sales training, events and paid media
Tier 2 A significant feature that changes how buyers use the product Blog post, email to customers and prospects, sales briefing and in-app notice
Tier 3 A minor improvement or fix Release notes and an in-app message

Launch Checklist

Phase Work to finish
Pre-launch (6 to 8 weeks out) Positioning approved, pricing set, beta feedback gathered, launch content drafted, sales and support trained.
Launch week Announcement live, campaigns running, sales outreach started and a daily check on early signals.
Post-launch (first 90 days) Weekly KPI review, win/loss interviews, messaging fixes and a 90-day report to leadership.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • A date before readiness - Announcing before sales and support are trained turns early interest into poor first experiences.
  • Every launch at full volume - Treating small updates as major launches teaches the audience to ignore your announcements.
  • No owner after launch week - Without someone accountable for the first 90 days, the follow-up work stops once the excitement fades.
  • Channels picked by habit - Spending on channels the team always uses, rather than where the buyer looks, wastes the launch budget.
