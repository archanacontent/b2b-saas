Performance And Optimisation

Ninety days after launch, someone in leadership asks whether it worked. Without targets agreed in advance, the answer becomes a debate. Marketing points to sign-ups, sales points to a thin pipeline, and product points to usage that nobody defined as success.

Measurement settles the question before it's asked. You agree on a small set of metrics during planning, track them on a shared scorecard and feed what you learn from won and lost deals back into the positioning. The same loop tells you which parts of the launch to repeat next time.

You'll leave with a KPI framework, a launch scorecard and a win/loss analysis process that keeps the messaging honest.

Win/Loss Analysis

Lost deals hold the most useful feedback a launch will get, and most teams never ask for it. Interview buyers within 30 days of a decision, while the reasons are fresh, and use someone other than the rep who ran the deal.

Mistakes To Avoid

  • Metrics chosen after launch - Targets set once the results are in will always look met.
  • Activity counted as success - Page views and sign-ups mean little until they show up as pipeline and paid accounts.
  • Win/loss run by the rep - Buyers soften their reasons when talking to the person who sold to them.
  • Findings with no owner - A theme that reaches no team gets repeated in the next launch.
